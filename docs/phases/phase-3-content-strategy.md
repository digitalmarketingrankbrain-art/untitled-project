## 0. Writing Style Guide

**Voice:** An institution speaking plainly, not a brand speaking persuasively. Every sentence should survive the test: *"Would this still be true and specific with the marketing adjectives removed?"*

**Do:**
- State what is true and specific ("Assessors are assigned based on documented competence in the relevant scope" beats "world-class assessors").
- Explain process and criteria, not just outcomes.
- Use active voice and short sentences for anything procedural (how to apply, what happens next).
- Name the thing precisely (accreditation number, scope, effective date) rather than gesturing at it.
- Let evidence carry the trust claim — link to the actual policy, the actual verification tool, the actual governance page — instead of asserting trustworthiness in prose.

**Avoid:**
- Superlatives without a source ("world's leading," "most trusted," "globally recognised").
- Vague authority claims ("internationally accredited," "government approved") unless confirmed and sourced.
- Sales-pitch framing on informational pages (governance, policy, process pages describe; they don't persuade).
- Stacking adjectives in front of nouns ("industry-leading, world-class, cutting-edge accreditation services").
- Fake urgency or scarcity language — inappropriate for an institutional/regulatory-adjacent context.

**Placeholder convention:** Any factual claim not confirmed by the user is written as `[PLACEHOLDER — REQUIRES CONFIRMATION: <what's needed>]` inline, so it's visually obvious in review and can't accidentally ship.

## 1. Page-by-Page Content Strategy

### 1.1 Homepage
- **Objective:** Answer "who are you, what do you do, why should I trust you, what do I do next" within one scroll for each of the three main audience types (applicant, verification user, general visitor).
- **Audience:** All — this page has to triage, not convert everyone.
- **Primary message:** We accredit [scope categories] against defined criteria, through a documented and verifiable process.
- **Primary CTA:** Verify an accreditation (highest-frequency, lowest-friction action).
- **Secondary CTA:** Start an application / explore programs.
- **Trust signals:** Verification tool front-and-centre, governance link, plain process explanation, dated content, no overclaiming.
- **Required supporting info:** Link out to Programs, How It Works, Verify, Governance, Resources.

### 1.2 Accreditation Overview + How It Works
- **Objective:** Let a prospective applicant self-qualify and understand the process without contacting anyone.
- **Audience:** New applicants, general visitors evaluating credibility.
- **Primary message:** Accreditation is a defined, staged process with clear criteria — not a paid rubber stamp.
- **Primary CTA:** View programs / Start application.
- **Secondary CTA:** Download fee guidance or process document.
- **Trust signals:** Named stages, named decision point separate from assessment, link to complaints/appeals for when things don't go as expected.
- **Required supporting info:** Stage-by-stage timeline, what's assessed, who decides, what happens on a negative decision.

### 1.3 Program detail (template, ×5 placeholder programs)
- **Objective:** Let a visitor determine in under a minute whether this scope applies to their organisation.
- **Audience:** New applicants.
- **Primary message:** Precisely what this scope covers and requires — not marketing language.
- **Primary CTA:** Apply for this program.
- **Secondary CTA:** View eligibility criteria / fees.
- **Trust signals:** Explicit scope boundaries (what's included/excluded), reference to the standard/criteria used.
- **Required supporting info:** Eligibility, standard/criteria referenced, typical timeline, required documents.

### 1.4 Verification search + detail
- **Objective:** Deliver an unambiguous yes/no/status answer with zero friction.
- **Audience:** Relying parties (verification users) — most will never visit any other page.
- **Primary message:** Search, get a definitive current status.
- **Primary CTA:** Search.
- **Secondary CTA:** Report suspected fraud (only shown if status is concerning or not found).
- **Trust signals:** No login required; explicit status labels; never a bare "Verified."
- **Required supporting info:** See Phase 7 for full status-state design.

### 1.5 About: Who We Are / Governance / Impartiality & Ethics
- **Objective:** Let a skeptical visitor (press, regulator-adjacent, competitor, careful applicant) satisfy themselves this is a legitimate, well-governed body.
- **Audience:** General visitor/stakeholder, careful applicants.
- **Primary message:** Described governance structure, named decision-making body, documented impartiality safeguards.
- **Primary CTA:** None forced — this page's job is to be read and believed, not to convert.
- **Secondary CTA:** Contact / read policies.
- **Trust signals:** Everything here IS the trust signal — structure, named roles (not necessarily named individuals unless confirmed), conflict-of-interest policy link.
- **Required supporting info:** `[PLACEHOLDER — REQUIRES CONFIRMATION]` for governance structure specifics, legal entity details, any real credentials.

### 1.6 Resources (Policies / Procedures / Forms)
- **Objective:** Self-service access to the documents applicants and accredited orgs actually need.
- **Audience:** Applicants, accredited organisations, researchers/press.
- **Primary message:** Nothing here is hidden behind a sales conversation.
- **Primary CTA:** Download/view document.
- **Trust signals:** Version numbers, effective dates on every document.

### 1.7 Training
- **Objective:** Let a participant decide to register without a call.
- **Audience:** Training participants (assessor candidates, client-org staff).
- **Primary message:** What you'll learn, who it's for, what you receive.
- **Primary CTA:** Register / enquire.
- **Trust signals:** Named prerequisites, clear format/dates, no inflated outcome claims (avoid implying a certificate of attendance confers authority it doesn't).

### 1.8 News & Notices
- **Objective:** Demonstrate the organisation is active and that status changes (including negative ones like withdrawals) are published, not hidden.
- **Audience:** All, especially verification-adjacent and press.
- **Primary message:** Current, dated, includes both routine and adverse notices.
- **Trust signals:** The presence of adverse notices (suspensions/withdrawals) is itself a credibility signal — a body that only ever publishes good news reads as curated, not transparent.

### 1.9 FAQs
- **Objective:** Deflect repetitive contact-form volume with real answers, segmented by audience.
- **Audience:** All four main segments.
- **Primary message:** Organised by who's asking, not a flat wall of Q&A.

### 1.10 Contact / Complaints & Appeals / Report Fraud
- **Objective:** Make sure each type of message reaches the right internal owner and the sender feels heard, not funneled into a void.
- **Audience:** All, but especially the two highest-trust-value flows: complaints/appeals (Phase 1 problem #5) and fraud reporting (problem #6).
- **Primary message (Complaints & Appeals):** There is a defined, fair process for challenging a decision or raising a concern about impartiality/competence.
- **Primary message (Report Fraud):** We take misuse seriously and investigate reports.
- **Trust signals:** Described process and timeline expectations, not just a form.
